05 / Why Hiblaya

We weave together stories.

Hiblaya is a home for Philippine fashion. It showcases local brands, designers and makers, and follows the trends shaping how Filipinos dress today.

The name joins hibla (to weave) and laya (freedom). It reflects our belief that everyone should be free to wear what they like, because clothes are one of the most personal ways to express who we are.

We weave together the stories of local labels, the styles taking off on the streets and social media, and the people behind them.

RICHBOYZ:
Actually Good,
or Just Good Marketing?

The Filipino streetwear brand that somehow became both a flex and a fashion debate.

Philippine Streetwear · Local Brand · Honest Review

There is something fascinating about Richboyz.

You see the logo. You see the oversized silhouette. You see the ₱1,600 tee or the ₱3,300-ish hoodie. Then comes the inevitable question:

“Is this actually worth it… or am I just paying for the name?”

Richboyz has become one of those local brands that people have opinions about. Some buyers praise its comfort, silhouettes, and overall aesthetic. Others think the branding is too loud and the prices don't match what they're getting. And honestly? Both conversations are worth having.

The ₱1,600 Question

Let's start with the numbers.

On Richboyz's current website, many of its tees are around ₱1,400–₱1,600, while hoodies commonly sit around ₱3,100–₱3,500. Its current catalog also includes denim, jackets, knitwear, accessories, and women's pieces.

₱1,600
THE TEE QUESTION

So no, it's not exactly “I found this for ₱399 at the mall” territory.

But Richboyz isn't positioning itself as bargain clothing either. The brand describes itself as “luxury streetwear essentials, made in the Philippines.”

The Good: People Actually Like Wearing It

There is plenty of positive customer feedback.

Richboyz's Trustpilot page currently shows 4.5/5 from 25 reviews, although those reviews are mostly from 2024 and Trustpilot currently shows no reviews in the last 12 months, so that score shouldn't be treated as a complete picture of its current reputation.

Customer reviews on the brand's own review platform also praise the feel, appearance and fit of different pieces, including hoodies and tees.

The aesthetic is largely built around:

oversized silhouettes + muted colors + casual streetwear + recognizable branding.

Basically, clothes that can go from “I'm going to class.” to “Coffee in BGC.” to “Wait, we're going out tonight?” without requiring an entire outfit change.

But Here's Where It Gets Spicy 🌶️️

The internet is not universally impressed.

Recent Filipino fashion discussions have criticized Richboyz for its branding, perceived price-to-quality ratio, and the simplicity of some designs. Other commenters defended individual pieces and said some silhouettes are genuinely good.

And honestly, that's probably the most interesting thing about Richboyz.

“Does the design justify the price?”

That's a completely different question.

A ₱1,600 shirt can be worth ₱1,600 to one person and absolutely not worth it to another.

Fashion has never been purely about fabric.

You're also buying design. Fit. Branding. Identity. Community.

And, let's be brutally honest—the feeling of wearing something other people recognize.

The Biggest Problem? The Name. 😭

Let's talk about the elephant in the room.

RICHBOYZ.

The name itself has become part of the conversation.

Some Filipino fashion commenters love the aesthetic but dislike the branding, especially the prominent logo treatment. Others associate the brand with a particular type of young streetwear consumer. Those are opinions from online communities, not objective facts about every Richboyz customer.

And this is where fashion gets weird.

Because a logo isn't simply a logo anymore.

It's a social signal.

Wear one brand and people might read you as minimalist. Another and you're suddenly “streetwear.” Another and you're giving “old money.” And another?

You're apparently a Young Stunna. 💀

Whether those stereotypes are fair is another conversation. But they demonstrate something important:

Richboyz has successfully created a recognizable identity.

You don't have to love that identity to recognize it.

The Price Debate Is Real

One of the more recent Reddit discussions compared Richboyz, Charlotte Folk and Toughwave hoodies. A commenter reported paying approximately ₱3,500 for a Richboyz hoodie, while criticizing its perceived value compared with the other brands. Other commenters similarly described Richboyz as expensive for what they considered relatively simple designs.

Meanwhile, another Filipino discussion described Richboyz as good quality but not particularly affordable, illustrating that opinions about value aren't unanimous.

The fairest question isn't “Is it too expensive?”

Ask instead: “Is THIS particular piece worth ₱1,600 or ₱3,500 to me?”

So… Should You Buy It? 👀

Don't buy Richboyz because TikTok told you that you need it.

Don't buy it because your friend has one.

And definitely don't buy it simply because you think wearing the logo will magically make an outfit look expensive.

Look at the actual garment.

Check the material. Check measurements. Look at the construction. Think about how many outfits you can make with it.

And most importantly:

Would you still want it if the logo disappeared?

That question changes EVERYTHING.

If the answer is yes, you're probably buying the clothes.

If the answer is no, you're probably buying the branding.

And neither is inherently wrong. That's literally what fashion does.

The Final Fit Check 🪞

Richboyz isn't some mysterious scam brand hiding behind a fancy website.

It's an established Filipino streetwear label with a substantial current catalog, customer feedback that includes significant praise, and a recognizable aesthetic.

But it also has a polarizing identity and an ongoing debate around whether some pieces justify their prices. Recent Filipino fashion discussions show that divide pretty clearly.

So perhaps the real Richboyz question isn't:

Is it good?

“Is it good enough FOR YOU?”

Because at the end of the day, fashion isn't a courtroom.

You don't need Reddit to approve your outfit.

You just need to look in the mirror and think:

“Yeah. I'd wear this.”
